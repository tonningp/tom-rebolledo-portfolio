A useful approach is:

1. **Session 1:** Define 2–3 SMART goals.
2. **Sessions 2–5:** Monitor progress and adjust steps.
3. **Session 6:** Evaluate outcomes and define next-stage goals.

In other words, the SMART goals become the **spine of the entire mentoring sequence**.

Below is a practical way to structure this for your two mentees.

---

# SMART Goals Overview

SMART stands for:

**Specific**
Clearly states what will be done.

**Measurable**
Progress can be evaluated objectively.

**Achievable**
Realistic within the mentoring timeframe.

**Relevant**
Directly related to the student’s academic or career pathway.

**Time-Bound**
Has a defined deadline.

For this program, the deadline naturally becomes **Session 6 (May 29, 2026)**.

---

# Tom Rebolledo

Career Path: Cybersecurity
Transfer Goal: CSUSB

Tom’s goals should emphasize **technical exposure + career awareness + transfer preparation**.

## SMART Goal 1 — Technical Skill Development

**Goal Statement**

By May 29, Tom will complete **three introductory cybersecurity lab exercises** using Linux tools or networking utilities and document them in a GitHub repository.

Why it satisfies SMART:

Specific
Cybersecurity labs using Linux/networking tools.

Measurable
Three labs documented.

Achievable
Three small labs over ~10 weeks is realistic.

Relevant
Directly aligned with cybersecurity.

Time-Bound
Deadline: May 29.
